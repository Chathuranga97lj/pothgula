01

Purpose and scope

Pothgula replaces the paper register. It keeps one shared record of every book, every member and every loan across all of your branches, tells your staff what needs attention today, and keeps a permanent record of who did what.

What the system does

  • Holds the catalogue of every book copy the library owns, each with a number that says which branch accessioned it and which category it belongs to.
  • Records who borrowed what and when it is due back, lets a member ask for two more weeks, and shows staff what is due soon and what is overdue.
  • Lets a member request a book held at another branch and tracks that book on its way there and back.
  • Settles lost books — the member either replaces the exact book or pays an amount staff decide, within 100 days.
  • Takes membership applications from the public, holds them until a librarian approves, and tracks each membership year and its fee.
  • Publishes each branch's opening hours, notices and events to members.
  • Shows every warning and reminder on the dashboard of the person who needs to see it.
  • Keeps an activity log of every action by every person, and exports it and every report as text and PDF.
  • Produces summaries — most and least borrowed books, stock, fees collected, missing books.

What it does not do

This release is a lending and membership system, not a full library automation package. It does not do acquisitions and purchase orders, loans to or from other libraries, digital or e-book lending, online fee payment, or a public catalogue open to non-members. It also sends nothing outside itself — no SMS, no WhatsApp message, no email, no push notification. Section 19 explains the consequences of each of these; section 20 shows where they could be added later.

A constraint that shapes everything below

Pothgula is a free and open-source project. Nothing in it may depend on a paid third-party service, or on a charge per message, per request or per user. That decision removed SMS reminders in version 2.0; in this version it decides how notice images and branch logos are stored (section 12) and rules out a WhatsApp gateway even though the WhatsApp number is now the sign-in name. It is written down as requirement NFR-11 so that it cannot be quietly reversed later.

How to read this document

Every requirement has an identifier such as FR-CIR-04 and a priority. Must means the system is not accepted without it. Should means it is planned and will be dropped only if we agree to drop it. Could means it is desirable and will be built if time allows. Requirements that are new or altered since version 2.0 are marked New or Changed; those removed are marked Withdrawn and greyed out. Please mark any requirement you disagree with before signing section 22.

03

Who uses the system

Three kinds of people use Pothgula, plus one temporary state: an applicant who has registered but not yet been approved. Everyone signs in with their WhatsApp number and a password.

Member

The person who borrows books

Applies once, is approved by a librarian, pays an annual fee, and belongs to one home branch.

  • Browses the catalogue of every branch and sees what is on the shelf
  • Borrows at their home branch; requests books from other branches
  • Asks for a two-week extension on a book they hold
  • Sees due dates, what they owe, opening hours, notices and events in one panel
  • Changes their own password and their own contact details
Admin · Librarian

Runs the counter at one branch

Day-to-day staff. Everything they do is limited to their own branch, and they cannot delete.

  • Approves membership applications; issues books and takes them back
  • Decides extension requests and handles other-branch requests
  • Adds and edits books, registers members, publishes notices and events
  • Records annual fees and lost-book settlements, and sets the lost-book amount
  • Sets opening hours; reads and exports the branch's log
Super admin

Runs the library organisation

Everything an admin can do, across every branch, plus the things that must not be undone by mistake.

  • Creates branches with their two-letter codes, sets branch names and logos, defines categories
  • Creates staff accounts; sets the annual fee for each branch
  • Deletes records, waives lost-book charges, moves books between branches permanently
  • Reads the activity log and the summaries for the whole library

The applicant column

An applicant is not a fourth role. It is a member whose application has not yet been approved. What this settles, and what it leaves

Approval closes the largest risk in version 2.0 — an unknown person on the internet creating a live member and walking out with books (risk D2). The public form stays, so nobody has to type at the counter what the applicant can type at home; the librarian's job becomes checking and pressing Approve. What is not yet decided is whether the first year's fee is taken at the moment of approval (question Q6 in section 21) and who exactly the "person who introduced them" is — a guarantor, a referee, or simply a note (Q5).

Still open from version 1.0

FR-AUT-04 means the super admin code is the single thing standing between the internet and full control of your library data. Recommendation R5 in section 18 proposes a safer arrangement at no extra cost, and R4 still proposes dropping the Member / Admin / Super admin choice from the sign-in screen.

The new ceiling

Two letters give 676 branches; a letter and a digit give 260 categories; five digits give 99,999 books per branch per category. Version 2.0's 26-branch limit (risk D10) is gone. The categories are the number to watch now: 260 is plenty for a public library's own scheme, but it is not enough to mirror a standard classification such as Dewey, which was never the intention (risk D9).

06

The book catalogue

Every physical book on your shelves is one entry in the catalogue, identified by a number written or stuck on the book itself. The number changes shape again in this version, to make room for more branches and more categories.

The accession number

Eleven characters, always the same shape: KS-A1-01234.

KS

Branch code

Two capital letters, given to the branch when it is created. Unique across the library and never reused.

A1

Category code

One capital letter and one digit — the book's category, each with a readable name.

01234

Running number

Five digits, counting up from 00001 within that branch and that category. The system allocates it, not the librarian.

Why the pictures live inside the system

You asked whether images could be "hard-coded or kept on a free service". A word on the five-day list

With a 14-day loan, a book spends more than a third of its life "due within five days". The consequence, stated plainly

R1 Separate the title from the copy. Five copies of the same book mean typing the writer, publisher and title five times, and a typo in one splits the book into two entries in search results. R7 Record a due date and a separate returned date on each loan. One date field whose meaning changes makes "which books are overdue right now" impossible to answer reliably. Adopted — v1.0
R8 Decide the rule for other branches. Decided: a member borrows at home and requests from elsewhere; the book travels. Section 8. Adopted — v3.0
R9 Make super admin an organisation-wide role, and admin the branch-scoped one. Super admins now set branch names and logos, define categories, set every branch's fee and see every branch's requests, all of which are organisation-wide acts. Yet FR-AUT-04 still caps them at five per branch. Open
R10 Add barcode or QR scanning at the counter once the basics are live. The eleven-character accession number is fixed-length and unambiguous, and a USB scanner or a phone camera reads it faster than anyone can type it. Your notes mark this for research; FR-CAT-10 and FR-CAT-17 hold the place. Open — phase 3
R11 Decide the branch-letter ceiling now. Decided: two letters. Section 5. Adopted — v3.0
R12 Implement "delete" as withdraw everywhere, even for super admins. If a super admin can still remove a book outright, the loans and requests that pointed at it are orphaned and the reports that counted it silently change. Open
R13 Write the activity log in the same transaction as the change it describes. If the log is written afterwards, an interruption between the two leaves a change with no record. Open
R14 Record the phone number of the person who introduced the member, not only their name. A name alone is a note; a name with a number is someone the library can call when a member with a lost book stops answering. If the introducer is meant to vouch for the member, the number is what makes that real. If they are only a note, the name is enough — question Q5. New in v3.0
R15 When the 100-day window passes, suspend rather than cancel. Cancelling a membership loses the loan history and the settlement with it, and the member simply applies again under the other phone number. Blocking every borrowing feature while keeping the account, as FR-LST-15 does, keeps the debt attached to the person. New in v3.0
R16 Keep the five-day list as a working list, not as alerts. It will be the longest thing on the staff screen every day, and most of what is on it is fine. If every line were an alert, the overdue books — the ones that actually need a phone call — would be lost among them. A list that staff can sort by due date, with the overdue books held separately above it, keeps the panel honest. New in v3.0
R17 Write a tariff for lost-book amounts and give it to every librarian. Letting staff set the amount is right for a donated book with a 1990 price on it. But two librarians will set two different amounts for the same book unless there is a written rule — a table by category, or "current shop price, or twice the recorded price, whichever is higher". The system suggests a figure; the tariff is what makes the suggestion consistent between branches. New in v3.0
19

Limitations and risks

What this system will not do for you, and what could go wrong. We would rather you know now than discover it after handover. Two risks from version 2.0 are closed by this update; five are new.

Serious — please decide on these before we build

Anyone with the code becomes an owner

D1

The super admin registration code is a single shared secret with no expiry. A staff member who leaves keeps the ability to create a super admin account, read every member's details, delete records and waive charges.

Mitigation: adopt R5. Otherwise, rotate the code whenever anyone leaves and accept the risk in writing.

Unverified members can borrow real books

D2

Closed in v3.0. Self-registration now produces an application, and nothing can be borrowed until a librarian approves it (FR-AUT-16, FR-AUT-17).

Late is still free; only lost costs money

D3

A member can now ask for two more weeks, which is the honest route. But a member who simply keeps a book for a year still owes nothing as long as they eventually bring it back, and nothing in the system stops them borrowing again meanwhile. Version 3.0 calls out the lateness at the counter, which helps; it does not put a price on it.

Mitigation: tell us your fine policy and we will cost it. Blocking further borrowing while a book is overdue costs almost nothing and would close most of this gap on its own. Question Q14.

The counter stops when the internet does

D4

This is a web system with no offline mode. During an outage at a branch, staff cannot issue or take back books and must record transactions on paper, then enter them later.

Mitigation: a printed daily loans sheet as a fallback, plus an agreed procedure for entering a backlog. Offline-capable software is a much larger project.

Reminders reach only people who sign in — permanently

D6

A member who never signs in never learns their book is due, that their extension was declined, that a requested book is waiting for them, or that the 100-day window is about to close. Making the WhatsApp number the sign-in name does not change this: the system still cannot send a WhatsApp message.

Mitigation: the call list in FR-NOT-04 and the five-day list in FR-NOT-12, and staff time on the telephone or WhatsApp by hand. Plan for someone to do this weekly.

The lost-book amount is now a judgement

D14

Two members losing the same book at two branches can be charged two different amounts, decided by two different librarians, and the log will show it. That is a fairness complaint waiting to happen, and in a small community it will be made in person.

Mitigation: R17 — a written tariff. The system's suggestion (FR-LST-02) and the super admin's power to adjust (FR-LST-09) exist so that departures from the tariff are visible and correctable. Question Q2.

A book between two branches belongs to nobody

D15

The system knows a book left branch GL and has not arrived at branch KS. It does not know who is carrying it, and if it never arrives there is no member to settle with — the loss falls on the library and the log can only say which staff member pressed Dispatch.

Mitigation: a written transport procedure — who carries, how often, signed for at each end — and FR-REQ-12 flagging anything in transit too long. Question Q8.

Worth knowing

No reservations for books on loan

D5

A member can now request a book that is on the shelf at another branch. They still cannot queue for a book that is on loan, at any branch: they must keep checking, and whoever arrives first gets it.

Option: phase 3 feature; the request flow in section 8 is most of the machinery.

Books now move between branches — and staff must keep up

D7

The transit and hold states in section 8 are only as accurate as the staff who record dispatch and arrival. A book that arrived but was never marked as arrived sits "in transit" for ever, and the member who asked for it is never told it is ready.

Mitigation: FR-REQ-09 puts every undone step on the panel of the branch that owes it; FR-REQ-12 flags the stuck ones to super admins.

Personal data brings legal duties, and there is more of it now

D8

The record now holds two phone numbers, an organisation and the name of a third person who did not fill in the form. Staff at a second branch see all of it when a request arrives. Applicants who are rejected leave their details behind. Single-person exports (FR-RPT-13) put all of it in a file that can be carried anywhere.

Mitigation: NFR-09, FR-AUT-20's purge of unsuccessful applications, and logging every export. We will supply a privacy notice for the application form. The policy decisions are yours. Questions Q19 and Q20.

The catalogue is local, not standard

D9

Books are catalogued with your own codes rather than a standard classification. Simpler to use, but not importable into a standard library system without manual work — and the accession number now bakes your codes into every label.

Option: fill in the ISBN where the book has one; FR-CAT-16 makes that quicker.

The numbering scheme caps you at 26 branches

D10

Closed in v3.0. Two letters allow 676 branches and a letter and a digit allow 260 categories.

The log will become the biggest thing in the database

D11

Logging every change means the log grows without limit. Requests, extensions and approvals add entries on top of loans. It will outgrow every other table within the first year — except, possibly, the images (D18).

Mitigation: FR-LOG-11 archives entries past the retention period, and FR-LOG-12 keeps browsing out entirely. Question Q20.

A locked-out super admin has no self-service way back

D12

A super admin who forgets their password depends on another super admin to reset it.

Mitigation: FR-AUT-14 keeps two super admins alive. As a last resort we will supply a command run directly on the server, documented at handover.

Twice the price may not replace the book

D13

Superseded in v3.0. The doubled price is now only a suggestion and staff set the amount. The risk has moved: see D14.

The five-day list is long by design

D16

More than a third of all loans are always "due within five days". If staff learn to scroll past the list, they will scroll past the overdue books above it too.

Mitigation: R16. The number of days is a setting, so it can be shortened if the list proves more noise than help.

Applications that nobody approves

D17

An applicant who fills in the form and is never approved has a working sign-in, sees the catalogue, and can never borrow. They will not know whether they were rejected or forgotten, and the library holds their details all the while.

Mitigation: FR-AUT-18 counts them on every staff panel; FR-AUT-20 flags them after 30 days and purges after 90. The number of days is yours to set.

Pictures are the one thing that can fill the free database

D18

The technical decision record proposes a free database tier of 0.5 GB. Every record in this system put together will not reach a tenth of that. Images can: two hundred notices with an unresized 2 MB photograph each is 400 MB, and the daily backup carries all of it.

Mitigation: FR-PUB-05 and NFR-16 resize on upload to a bounded size — a notice picture at 1200 pixels wide is around 150 KB, which makes the same two hundred notices 30 MB. Withdrawn notices keep their images; a super admin's delete removes them. If notices are frequent, self-hosting on a real server (NFR-13) removes the ceiling altogether.

Delivery in three phases

We recommend putting a working counter system in your hands early rather than delivering everything at once. Each phase is usable on its own. The new features are placed where they depend least on what is not yet built.

1

The counter works

First release

Branches with their two-letter codes, names and logos; categories with their codes and names; accounts and sign-in by WhatsApp number; membership applications with approval, the pending list and the single-member export; password change and counter reset; the book catalogue with the new accession numbers and search; issuing and taking back with due dates and the late-return callout; the five-day list and the overdue list on the staff dashboard; membership expiry — and the activity log, from the first day. The log has to be there from the start: the actions it did not record are gone. At the end of this phase you can stop using the paper register.

2

The library runs itself

Second release

Extensions by request and approval; other-branch requests with transit and hold; the lost-book settlement with staff-set amounts and the 100-day window; opening hours and closures; notices and events; annual fee payments with history and receipts; the member-facing dashboard with its full notification panel; the log viewing screen with text, spreadsheet and PDF export; and the reports of section 15 with the same exports.

3

Faster and further

Once phases 1 and 2 are settled in use

Barcode and QR scanning at the counter and the shelf, filling in book details from an ISBN, reservations for books on loan, fines for late returns if you decide on them, and the year-end summary document. SMS and WhatsApp messaging are not on this list — they are out of scope by decision, not deferred.
