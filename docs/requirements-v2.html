01

Purpose and scope

Pothgula replaces the paper register. It keeps one shared record of every book, every member and every loan across all of your branches, tells your staff what needs attention today, and keeps a permanent record of who did what.

What the system does

  • Holds the catalogue of every book copy the library owns, each with a number that says which branch accessioned it and which category it belongs to.
  • Records who borrowed what and when it is due back, and shows overdue loans to staff.
  • Settles lost books — the member either replaces the exact book or pays twice its price.
  • Tracks membership — each member is valid for one year and is renewed when the annual fee is paid.
  • Publishes each branch's opening days and times to members.
  • Shows every warning and reminder on the dashboard of the person who needs to see it.
  • Keeps an activity log of every action by every person, staff and member, that both admins and super admins can read.
  • Produces summaries — most and least borrowed books, stock, fees collected, missing books.

What it does not do

This release is a lending and membership system, not a full library automation package. It does not do acquisitions and purchase orders, inter-library loans, digital or e-book lending, online fee payment, or a public catalogue open to non-members. It also sends nothing outside itself — no SMS, no email, no push notification. Section 16 explains the consequences of each of these; section 17 shows where they could be added later.

A constraint that shapes everything below

Pothgula is a free and open-source project. Nothing in it may depend on a paid third-party service, or on a charge per message, per request or per user. That single decision is what removed SMS reminders and phone-code password resets from this version, and it is written down as requirement NFR-11 so that it cannot be quietly reversed later.

How to read this document

Every requirement has an identifier such as FR-CIR-04 and a priority. Must means the system is not accepted without it. Should means it is planned and will be dropped only if we agree to drop it. Could means it is desirable and will be built if time allows. Requirements that are new or altered since version 1.0 are marked New or Changed; those removed are marked Withdrawn and greyed out. Please mark any requirement you disagree with before signing section 19.

03

Who uses the system

Three kinds of people use Pothgula. Everyone signs in with their phone number and a password.

Member

The person who borrows books

Registers once, pays an annual fee, and belongs to one home branch.

  • Browses the catalogue and sees what is on the shelf
  • Checks the books they hold, when they are due, and anything they owe
  • Sees opening hours, membership expiry and every alert in one panel
  • Changes their own password
Admin · Librarian

Runs the counter at one branch

Day-to-day staff. Everything they do is limited to their own branch, and they cannot delete.

  • Issues books and takes them back
  • Adds and edits books, and registers members
  • Records annual fee payments and lost-book settlements
  • Sets opening days and times, and reads the branch's activity log
Super admin

Runs the library organisation

Everything an admin can do, across every branch, plus the things that must not be undone by mistake.

  • Creates branches, assigns branch codes, creates staff accounts
  • Deletes records, waives lost-book charges, moves books between branches
  • Reads the activity log and the summaries for the whole library

Why these two changes belong together

Giving librarians the power to catalogue books and register members makes them far more useful at the counter, and it is what the job actually is. It also widens what a single mistaken or dishonest account can do. The two safeguards in this version are deliberate counterweights: an admin can create and correct but never delete, so nothing they touch disappears; and every action is logged (section 11), so anything they do can be found afterwards. We would not recommend the first change without the second.

What this means in practice

There is no "forgot my password" link that works on its own. A member who has forgotten their password comes to the counter, is recognised by a librarian, and is given a temporary one. That is slower for the member, but it costs nothing to run — and recognising someone in person is stronger evidence than a code sent to a phone number that may since have changed hands.

The one case this does not cover is a super admin locked out when no other super admin is available. FR-AUT-14 makes that unlikely; risk D12 in section 16 says what happens if it occurs anyway.

Still open from version 1.0

FR-AUT-04 means the super admin code is the single thing standing between the internet and full control of your library data. Recommendation R5 in section 15 proposes a safer arrangement at no extra cost, and R4 still proposes dropping the Member / Admin / Super admin choice from the sign-in screen.

05

The book catalogue

Every physical book on your shelves is one entry in the catalogue, identified by a number written or stuck on the book itself. In this version that number changes shape, so that two branches numbering their own new arrivals on the same afternoon can never produce the same number.

The new accession number

Seven characters, always the same shape: AA00001.

A

Branch code

One capital letter, given to the branch when it is created. Unique across the library and never reused.

A

Category letter

One capital letter, the book's category — the same letters you already use, each with a readable name.

00001

Running number

Five digits, counting up from 00001 within that branch and that category. The system allocates it, not the librarian.

ExampleReads as
AA00001Branch A, category A, the first book of that category accessioned at that branch.
AA00100Branch A, category A, the hundredth.
AB00001Branch A, category B — a different counter, so this is also a first book.
BA00001Branch B, category A. Numbered on the same day as AA00001 and still unique.

The ceiling this format has

One letter for the branch means 26 branches, one letter for the category means 26 categories, and five digits mean 99,999 books per branch per category. The digits are ample. The 26 branches are a real limit, and going past it later would mean renumbering books already on shelves, which is the one thing FR-CAT-14 exists to avoid. If there is any prospect of more than about fifteen branches, tell us now and we will use two letters for the branch (AAA00001) from the start — it costs nothing today and cannot be retrofitted cheaply. This is question Q3 in section 18.

Carried over from version 1.0

FR-CAT-01 adds a book title, which your original table did not include. Recommendation R1 in section 15 goes further and explains why the title should be stored separately from the physical copy — that recommendation is still open, and the new numbering scheme does not settle it either way.

Lost and damaged books

New in version 2.0. A member who loses a book has exactly two ways to put it right: bring the same book again, or pay twice what the book is worth. Until one of them happens, they cannot borrow anything else.

Two things to decide

How long does a member get to bring the book back? Your notes mention a two-month window in which the member may find or buy the same book before being asked for money. That is a sensible rule and we have not written it in as a requirement, because the length is your decision, not ours. Tell us the number of months and it becomes FR-LST-14. This is question Q5 in section 18.

What price do we double? The charge is only as good as the price on the copy. A book donated in 1990 with a printed price of Rs 45 gives a charge of Rs 90, which will not replace it. Risk D13 in section 16 sets this out, and question Q11 asks how you want to handle it.

The consequence, stated plainly

A member who never signs in will never see a reminder. Those are exactly the members who go overdue. In version 1.0 this was a cost question with SMS as the answer; in version 2.0 it is a settled design decision, so the answer has to be a human one: the call list in FR-NOT-04, and staff telephoning people. Risk D6 in section 16 puts numbers around what that means for your staff.

The activity log

New in version 2.0. Everything anybody does in this system that changes anything is written down, permanently, and both admins and super admins can read it. This is what makes it safe to let librarians catalogue and register.

IDRequirementPriority
FR-LOG-01Every action that creates, changes or removes data is written to the activity log, whoever performed it — member, admin or super admin.Must
FR-LOG-02Each entry records the date and time, the account that acted and its role, the branch, what was done, which record it was done to, the values before and after the change, and the network address of the device used.Must

Why FR-LOG-12 is there

You asked for a log of every user action. We have written it as every action that changes something, and deliberately excluded browsing and searching. Two reasons. First, size: a hundred members browsing produces tens of thousands of entries a day and the log becomes the largest thing in the database, making the entries that matter harder to find. Second, and more important, a record of which books each member looked at is a record of what they read and think — personal data of a kind the library has no reason to hold and a duty to protect under the Personal Data Protection Act. If you do want sign-in and browsing history kept as well, say so in question Q12 and we will cost it, but we recommend against it.

Quality requirements

These apply to the whole system rather than to one feature, and they are as much a part of acceptance as the features above.

What we recommend changing

These are our professional recommendations on the design as it stands. None is a defect in your thinking — they are the places where a decision that is reasonable today becomes expensive in two years. Three of the ten from version 1.0 are now settled; the rest are still open.

IDRecommendationWhy it mattersStatus
R1 Separate the title from the copy. Five copies of the same book mean typing the writer, publisher and title five times, and a typo in one splits the book into two entries in search results. Storing the title once and the accession numbers against it makes "3 of 5 copies available" possible — and it makes the lost-book rule easier, because "an identical copy" becomes a thing the system understands rather than something staff eyeball. Open
R2 Work out availability from the loans rather than storing it as a separate yes/no field. Two places recording the same fact will eventually disagree — a crash mid-transaction leaves a book marked on loan with no loan against it, and staff cannot issue a book that is physically in their hand. One source of truth removes the whole class of problem. Open
R3 Store phone numbers as text, not as numbers, and require them to be unique. Stored as a number, 0771234567 loses its leading zero. Since the phone number is also the sign-in name, duplicates must be impossible. Adopted — NFR-15
R4 Drop the Member / Admin / Super admin choice from the sign-in screen; keep it only for registration. The password already determines who someone is. Asking first only creates a way to fail — a librarian who picks Member gets told their password is wrong. It also confirms to a stranger that a given phone number belongs to an admin. Open
R5 Make the super admin code single-use. After the first super admin exists, further staff are created by an existing super admin, not by self-registration. A fixed code shared among staff spreads by WhatsApp and never expires. Anyone who ever sees it can create a super admin account for themselves, at any time, from anywhere. This matters more in version 2.0 than it did in version 1.0, because a super admin can now also delete records and waive charges. Open
R6 Have staff approve a member's self-registration at the counter before the membership becomes active, and start the year from that approval. Partly answered: librarians can now register members themselves, which is the safe path. But the public self-registration form still exists alongside it, so anyone on the internet can still create an active member account. Either close that form or make what it produces an application rather than a membership. Open
R7 Record a due date and a separate returned date on each loan. One date field whose meaning changes depending on whether the book is back makes "which books are overdue right now" impossible to answer reliably. Adopted — v1.0
R8 Decide the rule for other branches: can a member borrow a book held elsewhere, or only see it? The design lets members search other branches but never says what happens next. If they can only look, members will travel to a branch expecting a book; if they can borrow, we need transfer requests and a way to track a book in transit. Either is fine, but it must be decided before build. Open
R9 Make super admin an organisation-wide role, and admin the branch-scoped one. As written, super admins are capped at five per branch yet their job is to create branches — so the first one has no branch to belong to. Version 2.0 makes this sharper: super admins now assign branch codes and read every branch's log, both of which are organisation-wide acts. Open
R10 Add barcode scanning at the counter once the basics are live. The new seven-character accession number is a better barcode than the old one: fixed length, no ambiguity about the letter, and the first two characters tell a scanner-reading human where the book belongs. A USB scanner costs little and issuing becomes scan-scan-done. Open — phase 3
R11 Decide the branch-letter ceiling now, not later. Use two letters for the branch if there is any chance of more than about fifteen branches. One letter allows 26 branches. Going past 26 later means either an inconsistent numbering scheme or renumbering and relabelling books already on shelves. Widening the branch code costs nothing before the first book is catalogued and is expensive afterwards. New in v2.0
R12 Implement "delete" as withdraw everywhere, even for super admins. The whole point of stopping admins deleting is that history survives. If a super admin can still remove a book outright, the loans that pointed at it are orphaned and the reports that counted it silently change. Marking a record withdrawn achieves what deleting is meant to achieve without breaking anything that referred to it. New in v2.0
R13 Write the activity log in the same transaction as the change it describes. If the log is written afterwards, an interruption between the two leaves a change with no record — and the one change you most want logged is the one during which something went wrong. Writing both together means either both happen or neither does. New in v2.0
16

Limitations and risks

What this system will not do for you, and what could go wrong. We would rather you know now than discover it after handover.

Serious — please decide on these before we build

Anyone with the code becomes an owner

D1

The super admin registration code is a single shared secret with no expiry. A staff member who leaves keeps the ability to create a super admin account, read every member's phone number and address, delete records and waive charges.

Mitigation: adopt R5. Otherwise, rotate the code whenever anyone leaves and accept the risk in writing.

Unverified members can borrow real books

D2

Self-registration still creates an active member instantly. There is no check that the person exists, that the address is real, or that the fee was paid — but the books they take away are physical and unrecoverable, and the lost-book charge is only collectable from someone you can find.

Mitigation: adopt R6. Librarians can now register members at the counter, so closing the public form costs almost nothing.

Late is still free; only lost costs money

D3

Version 2.0 puts a price on losing a book but none on keeping it. A member can hold a book for a year, be told about it on their dashboard every day, and owe nothing as long as they eventually bring it back. Nothing in the system blocks them from borrowing again either.

Mitigation: tell us your fine policy and we will cost it. Blocking further borrowing while a book is overdue costs almost nothing and would close most of this gap on its own. Question Q4.

The counter stops when the internet does

D4

This is a web system with no offline mode. During an outage at a branch, staff cannot issue or take back books and must record transactions on paper, then enter them later — which is when mistakes happen, and when the activity log records the wrong times.

Mitigation: a printed daily loans sheet as a fallback, plus an agreed procedure for entering a backlog. Offline-capable software is a much larger project.

Reminders now reach only people who sign in — permanently

D6

In version 1.0 this was a cost trade-off with SMS as the paid answer. In version 2.0 it is a settled decision, so there is no software answer left. A member who never signs in never learns their book is due, their membership has lapsed, or that they owe for a lost book.

Mitigation: the call list in FR-NOT-04, and staff time on the telephone. Plan for someone to make those calls weekly; otherwise the dashboard warnings reach only the members who were never going to be a problem.

Worth knowing

No reservations or holds

D5

A member who wants a book that is on loan has no way to queue for it. They must keep checking the catalogue, and whoever happens to arrive first gets it.

Option: phase 3 feature, moderate effort.

Books cannot move between branches

D7

A super admin can reassign a book to another branch, but there is no request, no approval and no in-transit state. A book being carried between branches simply appears to be on the shelf at its destination — while its accession number still says it came from somewhere else, which is correct but will confuse staff.

Option: depends on the answer to R8. Whichever way, brief staff that the first letter is where a book was numbered, not where it lives.

Personal data brings legal duties, and there is more of it now

D8

Holding names, phone numbers, dates of birth and addresses makes the library a data controller under the Personal Data Protection Act No. 9 of 2022. The activity log adds a record of what each member did and when, which is also personal data, and it is kept for two years.

Mitigation: we will supply a privacy notice for the registration page covering the log and the retention period. The policy decisions are yours. Questions Q12 and Q13.

The catalogue is local, not standard

D9

Books are catalogued with your own letter categories rather than a standard classification. That is simpler to use, but the data cannot be imported into a standard library system later without manual work — and the accession number now bakes your branch and category letters into every label on every shelf.

Option: the optional ISBN field in FR-CAT-01 is cheap insurance. Fill it in where the book has one.

The numbering scheme caps you at 26 branches

D10

Branch letter plus category letter plus five digits allows 26 branches, 26 categories and 99,999 books per branch per category. The digits are ample and the ambiguity of version 1.0 is gone. The 26 branches are a hard ceiling, and so are the 26 categories.

Mitigation: adopt R11 and use two letters for the branch if growth beyond fifteen branches is at all likely. Decide before the first label is printed.

The log will become the biggest thing in the database

D11

Logging every change means the log grows without limit and never shrinks. At a few hundred loans a day across all branches it will outgrow every other table within the first year, and searching it gets slower as it grows.

Mitigation: FR-LOG-11 archives entries past the retention period, and FR-LOG-12 keeps browsing out of the log entirely. Both are already in this document; what we need from you is the retention period. Question Q13.

A locked-out super admin has no self-service way back

D12

With phone-code resets gone, a super admin who forgets their password depends on another super admin to reset it. If the library has only one, or the second has left, nobody inside the application can help.

Mitigation: FR-AUT-14 keeps two super admins alive. As a last resort we will supply a command run directly on the server by whoever administers it — documented at handover, and it leaves its own entry in the log.

Twice the price may not replace the book

D13

The lost-book charge is only as good as the price recorded against the copy. A book donated in 1990 with Rs 45 printed on it produces a charge of Rs 90; a book with no price at all falls back to a branch default that may be nothing like its worth. The rule is fair only where the prices are current.

Mitigation: set a sensible branch default, use the flag in FR-CAT-12 to find unpriced books, and let a super admin adjust a charge under FR-LST-09 where the recorded price is plainly wrong. Question Q11.

Delivery in three phases

We recommend putting a working counter system in your hands early rather than delivering everything at once. Each phase is usable on its own.

1

The counter works

First release

Branches with their branch letters, categories with their names, accounts and sign-in, password change and counter reset, the book catalogue with the new accession numbers and search, issuing and taking back with due dates, membership expiry, the staff dashboard showing today's overdue books — and the activity log, from the first day. The log has to be there from the start: it is the one thing that cannot be added later, because the actions it did not record are gone. At the end of this phase you can stop using the paper register.

2

The library runs itself

Second release

Opening hours and closures, annual fee payments with history and receipts, the lost-book settlement of section 7, the member-facing dashboard with its notification panel, the log viewing screen with filters and export, and the summaries and reports of section 12.

3

Faster and further

Once phases 1 and 2 are settled in use

Barcode scanning at the counter, reservations, fines for late returns, renewals, inter-branch requests, and the year-end summary document. SMS is no longer on this list — it is out of scope by decision, not deferred. We would rather choose from the rest after you have used the system for a few months than guess now.

Decisions we need from you

Each of these changes what we build. Answers to Q1 to Q5 are needed before development starts; Q3 in particular must be settled before a single label is printed.

  1. Can a member borrow a book held at another branch, or only see that it exists there? Carried from v1.0 Q1. Recommendation R8.
  2. Should the public self-registration form stay, now that librarians can register members at the counter? If it stays, does what it produces become a membership immediately or an application a librarian activates? Carried from v1.0 Q2. Recommendation R6.
  3. How many branches will there be, at launch and in ten years? One letter allows 26. If fifteen is plausible we should use two letters from the start. Who assigns the letters, and does branch A mean the main branch? New. Recommendation R11.
  4. Is there a fine for a book that is merely late rather than lost? If so, what is the daily rate and is there a maximum? Should a member with an overdue book be blocked from borrowing again? Carried from v1.0 Q4. Risk D3.
  5. How long does a member have to bring the exact same book before the doubled price is demanded? Your notes suggest two months. New. This becomes FR-LST-14.
  6. What is the annual membership fee, and is it the same at every branch? Carried from v1.0 Q6.
  7. How many books and members in total, roughly? This decides how the reports and the log are built. Carried from v1.0 Q7.
  8. Who will be the first super admin, who is the second required by FR-AUT-14, and who holds the setup code? Carried from v1.0 Q8.
  9. Should the interface be in English only, or English with Sinhala and Tamil? Carried from v1.0 Q10.
  10. What are your categories? We need the full list of letters with their readable names before anything can be catalogued, because the letter is now part of every book number. New.
  11. For books with no price, or with a price printed decades ago, what should the lost-book charge be? A branch default figure, or the price re-checked when a book is declared lost? New. Risk D13.
  12. Should member browsing and searching be logged as well as actions that change data? We recommend not — see the note under FR-LOG-12. New.
  13. How long should activity log entries be kept before archiving? We have assumed two years. New. Risk D11.
  14. Which open-source licence should the project carry, and who holds the copyright — the library, or you personally? New. NFR-12.
  15. Where will the system be hosted, and who will run the daily backup and test the restore? New. NFR-08, NFR-13.
