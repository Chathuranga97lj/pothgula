01

Purpose and scope

Pothgula replaces the paper register. It keeps one shared record of every book, every member and every loan across all of your branches, tells your staff what needs attention today, and keeps a permanent record of who did what.

What the system does

  • Holds the catalogue: each book title once, and every physical copy of it, each copy with a number that says which branch accessioned it and which category it belongs to.
  • Records who borrowed what and when it is due back, lets a member ask for two more weeks, and shows staff what is due soon and what is overdue.
  • Lets a member request a book held at another branch and tracks that copy on its way there and back.
  • Settles lost books — the member either replaces the same book or pays the amount on the branch's lost-book tariff, within 100 days, after which borrowing is suspended until it is settled.
  • Takes membership applications from the public, holds them until a librarian approves them in person, and tracks each membership year and its fee.
  • Publishes each branch's opening hours, notices and events to members.
  • Shows every warning and reminder on the dashboard of the person who needs to see it.
  • Keeps an activity log of every change by every person, and exports it and every report as text, spreadsheet-ready CSV and PDF.
  • Produces summaries — most and least borrowed books, stock, fees collected, missing books.

What it does not do

This release is a lending and membership system, not a full library automation package. It does not do acquisitions and purchase orders, loans to or from other libraries, digital or e-book lending, online fee payment, fines for late returns, barcode or QR scanning, or a public catalogue open to non-members. It also sends nothing outside itself — no SMS, no WhatsApp message, no email, no push notification. Section 19 explains the consequences of each of these; section 20 shows which could be added later.

A constraint that shapes everything below

Pothgula is a free and open-source project. Nothing in it may depend on a paid third-party service, or on a charge per message, per request or per user. That decision removed SMS reminders in version 2.0, decides how notice images and branch logos are stored (section 12), and rules out a WhatsApp gateway even though the WhatsApp number is the sign-in name. It is written down as requirement NFR-11 so that it cannot be quietly reversed later.

How to read this document

Every requirement has an identifier such as FR-CIR-04 and a priority. Must means the system is not accepted without it. Should means it is planned and will be dropped only if we agree to drop it. Could means it is desirable and will be built if time allows. Requirements that are new or altered since version 3.0 are marked New or Changed; those removed are marked Withdrawn and greyed out. Please mark any requirement you disagree with before signing section 22.

03

Who uses the system

Three kinds of people use Pothgula, plus two passing states of a member's account: an applicant who has applied but not yet been approved, and a suspended member whose lost-book settlement has run past its window. Everyone signs in with their WhatsApp number and a password, on the same screen.

Member

The person who borrows books

Applies once, is approved in person by a librarian, pays an annual fee, and belongs to one home branch.

  • Browses the catalogue of every branch and sees what is on the shelf
  • Borrows at their home branch; requests books from other branches
  • Asks for a two-week extension on a book they hold
  • Sees due dates, what they owe, opening hours, notices and events in one panel
  • Changes their own password and their own contact details
Admin · Librarian

Runs the counter at one branch

Day-to-day staff. Everything they do is limited to their own branch.

  • Approves membership applications in person; issues books and takes them back
  • Decides extension requests and handles other-branch requests
  • Adds titles and copies, registers members, publishes notices and events
  • Records annual fees and lost-book settlements; keeps the branch's lost-book tariff
  • Sets opening hours; reads and exports the branch's log
Super admin

Runs the library organisation

ChangedBelongs to no single branch. Everything an admin can do, at every branch, plus the decisions that should rest with few people.

  • Creates branches with their two-letter codes, sets branch names and logos, defines categories
  • Creates every staff account, including other super admins; sets the annual fee for each branch
  • Voids mistaken loans, waives or reduces lost-book charges, lifts suspensions, moves copies between branches permanently, merges duplicate titles
  • Reads the activity log and the summaries for the whole library

What this settles

With no super admin code, the single most serious risk in v3.0 — anyone holding the code could make themselves an owner of the library's data (risk D1) — is gone rather than mitigated. With approval in person, a membership now means a real person, checked against a document, who has paid. And with anonymisation instead of deletion, the library can honour the personal data law without breaking the "nothing is ever deleted" rule it chose in R12.

The book catalogue

ChangedThe catalogue now has two levels. A title is the book as a reader thinks of it — its name, writer, publisher and category — and is entered once. A copy is one physical book on a shelf, with its own accession number, branch and condition. Five copies of the same novel are one title and five copies. (R1)

The accession number

Every copy carries one. Eleven characters, always the same shape: KS-A1-01234.

KS

Branch code

Two capital letters, given to the branch when it is created. Unique across the library and never reused.

A1

Category code

One capital letter and one digit — the title's category, each with a readable name.

01234

Running number

Five digits, counting up from 00001 within that branch and that category. The system allocates it, not the librarian.

Why the pictures live inside the system

A free image-hosting site is a paid service with the price set to zero for now: its terms change, its free tier shrinks, and every picture on every notice breaks on the same day. Limitations and risks

What this system will not do for you, and what could go wrong. We would rather you know now than discover it after handover. This version closes the most serious risk of v3.0, narrows four others, and adds one.

Serious — read before signing

Anyone with the code becomes an owner

D1

Closed in v3.1. There is no super admin code any more. Staff accounts can only be created by a super admin, and the first one only on the server itself (FR-AUT-04).

The counter stops when the internet does

D4

This is a web system with no offline mode. During an outage at a branch, staff cannot issue or take back books in the system.

Mitigation, now in the requirements: the daily loans sheet printed at opening (FR-RPT-16), paper slips during the outage, and back-dated entry within seven days afterwards (FR-CIR-14). Offline-capable software remains a much larger project.

Reminders reach only people who sign in — permanently

D6

A member who never signs in never learns their book is due, that their extension was declined, that a requested book is waiting for them, or that the 100-day window is about to close. The system cannot send a WhatsApp message.

Mitigation: the call list in FR-NOT-04 and the five-day list in FR-NOT-12, and staff time on the telephone or WhatsApp by hand. Plan for someone at each branch to do this weekly.

Late is free, but no longer unlimited

D3

Narrowed in v3.1. There is still no money fine for lateness — by decision, not omission. But a member holding an overdue book can no longer borrow, request or extend (FR-CIR-02), and repeat lateness is marked at the counter (FR-CIR-15).

If that is not enough: fines can be added later; they would need cash handling, receipts and a cap, and are quoted separately.

The same lost book can cost different amounts at different branches

D14

Narrowed in v3.1. Within a branch the tariff makes the amount consistent, and any departure carries a written reason. Between branches, amounts differ because each branch keeps its own tariff — your answer to Q2.

Mitigation: super admins can compare the branch tariffs side by side and agree a common one at any time without software changes.

A book between two branches belongs to nobody

D15

Decided in v3.1. A copy lost in transit is the library's loss, written off by a super admin; no member is charged (FR-REQ-14). The signed transit slip (FR-REQ-13) shows whose hands it passed through.

Still needed: the library adopts the transport procedure recommended in §8, or its own.

Bringing the existing books in is now a two-level job

D19

NewWith titles and copies separated, the import of the books already on your shelves must group copies into titles. Old registers spell the same title several ways; every misspelling becomes a separate title unless someone catches it.

Mitigation: the import shows probable duplicates for review before it is committed (FR-CAT-13), and a super admin can merge titles afterwards (FR-CAT-18). Allow a librarian's time for the review; it is a one-off.

Worth knowing

No reservations for books on loan

D5

A member can request a book on the shelf at another branch, but cannot queue for a book that is on loan anywhere: they must keep checking, and whoever arrives first gets it.

Option: phase 3; the request flow in section 8 is most of the machinery.

Books moving between branches — staff must keep up

D7

The transit and hold states are only as accurate as the staff who record dispatch and arrival. A copy that arrived but was never marked as arrived sits "in transit" for ever, and the member who asked for it is never told it is ready.

Mitigation: FR-REQ-09 puts every undone step on the panel of the branch that owes it; FR-REQ-12 flags the stuck ones to super admins.

Personal data brings legal duties

D8

The record holds two phone numbers, an organisation, and the name and phone number of a third person who did not fill in the form. Staff at a second branch see all of it when a request arrives. Single-person exports put all of it in a file that can be carried anywhere.

Mitigation: NFR-09, the introducer's consent tick (FR-AUT-03), anonymisation of unsuccessful applicants (FR-AUT-20), and logging every export. We will supply the privacy notice for the form.

The catalogue is local, not standard

D9

Books are catalogued with your own codes rather than a standard classification. Simpler to use, but not importable into a standard library system without manual work — and the accession number bakes your codes into every label.

Option: fill in the ISBN on titles that have one; FR-CAT-16 makes that quicker.

The log will become the biggest thing in the database

D11

Logging every change means the log grows without limit and will outgrow every other table within the first year.

Mitigation: FR-LOG-11 archives entries after two years; FR-LOG-12 keeps browsing out entirely.

A locked-out super admin

D12

A super admin who forgets their password depends on another super admin to reset it.

Mitigation: FR-AUT-14 keeps two alive; the server command of FR-AUT-04 is the documented last resort.

The five-day list is long by design

D16

With a 14-day loan, more than a third of all loans are always "due within five days" — seventy or eighty lines a morning at two hundred loans a week.

Mitigation: R16 — a sortable list below the overdue books, never an alert per line. The number of days is a setting.

Applications need a visit

D17

An applicant who applies online must still come to the branch with proof of identity and the fee before they can borrow. Some will not, and the library holds their details meanwhile.

Mitigation: the applicant's banner says exactly what to bring (FR-AUT-16); staff see the waiting list (FR-AUT-18); unapproved applications are anonymised after 90 days (FR-AUT-20).

Pictures and storage

D18

Reduced in v3.1. The real installation runs on the library's own server (NFR-13, NFR-17), where storage is not a practical limit. Only the public demonstration sits on a small free database, and it holds invented data.

Mitigation: pictures are still resized on upload (FR-PUB-05), which keeps backups small.

Closed in earlier versions

D2 · D10 · D13

Unverified members borrowing (D2) and the 26-branch ceiling (D10) were closed in v3.0. The doubled price not replacing the book (D13) became D14 in v3.0 and is narrowed above.
